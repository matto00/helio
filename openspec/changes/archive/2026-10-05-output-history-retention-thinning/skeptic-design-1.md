### Non-blocking notes
1. **Concurrent instances**: two instances may run the global thin DELETE at the same moment. Postgres READ COMMITTED handles row overlap, but two multi-row DELETEs in one transaction can occasionally deadlock. The failure is logged and retried next interval, so this is harmless. Still, a `pg_try_advisory_xact_lock` at the start of the transaction (no migration needed) would skip the overlap entirely for one line of SQL. Consider adding it, or record why not.
2. `UserTier` has no `values` collection. When the fallback builds the "effective map over all three tiers", use an explicit enumeration that the compiler forces you to keep in sync. Examples are an exhaustive match, or `Seq(Free, Beta, Owner)` guarded by a test that round-trips `UserTier.fromString` for every DB-legal value. Do not hand-write the list in two places.
3. `OutputHistoryRetentionPrivilegedSpec`: follow `RlsPrivilegedDmlSpec`'s rule of never re-granting `helio_privileged` in the harness. Otherwise the new `pipelines`/`users` join's grant dependency is masked rather than proven.
4. The new `output-history-retention` delta spec carries a `## Purpose` section ahead of `## ADDED Requirements`. The validator accepts it, and archive should lift it into the new base spec. Check after archive that it lands correctly.
5. The design says the thinning scan waits inside the tick. Bounded row counts make this fine, but the executor should record the measured tick duration with the 40-day fixture as evidence, not just assert it.
